“It’s late,” said the foreman, and he looked at the clock over the door as if it had done something to him personally.
The press was cold that morning, and the type sat in its cases like rain waiting to fall. Nobody in the shop said a word about the proofs, which were late again, and the foreman walked the length of the room twice before he sat down.
Ink is patient. It waits in the tin for weeks and then, on the one day that matters, it goes on thick and dark and even, and the sheet comes off the platen looking as if it had always been printed.
